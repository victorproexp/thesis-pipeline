The Sociotechnical Imaginary of AI in Higher Education

This page integrates lexical value salience and topic-network structure across Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft documents in the 2023 to 2026 corpus. The analytical framing follows the thesis lens: sociotechnical imaginaries as publicly performed visions, expectational gaps between what is and what ought to be, and the connector-complementor tension in higher-education governance.

Sociotechnical Imaginary

Recurring value language and topic emphasis are interpreted as institutionally stabilized visions of desirable educational futures.

Expectational Gap

Weak representation of democratic public values is contrasted with strong infrastructural and performance-oriented framing.

Issue Management Dynamics

Discursive positioning is assessed in relation to potential, imminent, current, and critical issue stages around legitimacy and regulation.

Interpretive Guide and Analytical Limits

  • The heatmap indicates which democratic public values are foregrounded, backgrounded, or effectively absent by company.
  • The graph locates where these value patterns are anchored in broader topical clusters such as security, governance, productivity, and policy.
  • Strong infrastructural framing combined with weak public-value emphasis is treated as a candidate value-conformance gap in Mahon's sense.
  • Lexical frequency is interpreted as discursive salience, not as direct evidence of organizational commitment or implementation outcomes.
  • Findings should be triangulated with qualitative close reading, given differences in document genre and publication format across firms.
Context alignment: Jasanoff and Kim (2015), Marginson (2011), van Dijck et al. (2018), Coombs (2021), Mahon (2022), Mager and Katzenbach (2021).
